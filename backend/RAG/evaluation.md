# Evaluation of RPAnswerBot 

This document summarizes the evaluation carried out on the RPAnswerBot system. The primary objective of this evaluation was to understand how different chunking methods, retrieval strategies, and embedding models influence the overall quality of answers generated by the RAG pipeline.

---

## Overview

To evaluate the effectiveness of our Research Paper Question Answering system, we conducted several experiments using multiple research papers from different technical domains. During testing, the same set of questions was executed across every available configuration while only changing one component of the pipeline at a time. This allowed us to observe how each strategy behaved under identical conditions.

Our evaluation mainly focused on three important stages of the RAG pipeline:

- Different chunking configurations
- Multiple retrieval techniques
- Two embedding models

The goal was not only to measure answer accuracy but also to understand how well each approach handled complex reasoning, factual retrieval, citation quality, and situations where the requested information was absent from the uploaded documents.

---

## Chunking Strategies

Three different chunking configurations were implemented and evaluated.

### Strategy A (Chunk Size = 1200, Overlap = 200)

This strategy creates comparatively larger chunks while maintaining sufficient overlap between consecutive chunks. During testing, it preserved complete paragraphs and reduced the possibility of splitting related concepts across multiple chunks. It generally produced stable responses for long explanations and research summaries.

### Strategy B (Chunk Size = 900, Overlap = 150)

This configuration generates smaller chunks with slightly lower overlap. Since the context is divided into finer sections, the retriever can locate highly specific information much faster. It performed particularly well for direct factual questions, definitions, and numerical values where precision was more important than surrounding context.

### Strategy C (Chunk Size = 1000, Overlap = 180)

The third strategy provides a balance between the previous two approaches. It preserves enough surrounding context while still maintaining focused chunks. Throughout our experiments, this configuration consistently delivered reliable results across different types of questions without favoring only one particular scenario.

---

## Retrieval Strategies

To compare retrieval quality, four different retriever configurations were integrated into the application.

### Similarity Search (Top-K = 3)

Similarity Search retrieves documents purely based on embedding similarity. It performs well when the user's query closely matches the wording used inside the research paper. However, in some cases it tends to retrieve multiple neighboring chunks containing similar information, leading to redundant context.

### MMR (Maximum Marginal Relevance)

MMR attempts to balance relevance with diversity by selecting chunks that are both useful and different from one another. This approach proved beneficial for questions that required information from multiple sections of a paper, such as combining methodology, experimental setup, and conclusions.

### Hybrid Retrieval

Hybrid Retrieval combines semantic vector search with traditional keyword-based BM25 retrieval. During testing, it demonstrated stronger robustness for technical terminology, abbreviations, equations, and uncommon keywords that may not always be captured effectively by embedding similarity alone. This combination produced consistently accurate retrieval across various document types.

### Reranker
The Reranker adds an additional ranking stage after the initial retrieval process. Instead of directly sending all retrieved chunks to the language model, it evaluates their relevance more precisely and selects only the strongest candidates. Although this introduces a slight increase in response time, it noticeably improves context quality and reduces unrelated information reaching the LLM. Here it is used with hybrid retrieval for efficient results.

## 8 Evaluation Queries & Observations
Below are the 8 specific queries used during testing and generalized observations from our pipeline.

## 1. Information Retrieval Basics
**Query 1**:"What is Information Retrieval (IR)?"
**Observation**:For this straightforward conceptual question, Strategy B combined with Similarity Search retrieved the exact definition efficiently. The smaller chunk size helped isolate the introductory explanation without including unnecessary surrounding context.


## 2. Concept Comparison
**Query 2**:"What is the difference between sparse retrieval and dense retrieval?"
**Observation**:Since the comparison spans multiple sections of the chapter, MMR and Hybrid Retrieval produced noticeably better answers than basic Similarity Search and reranker retrieval. Their ability to retrieve diverse yet relevant chunks resulted in more comprehensive explanations.

### 3. Missing Information Check (Hallucination Test)
- **Query 3**: "What is the use agentic AI?" (When the paper omits this detail)
- **Observation**: The RAG pipeline responded dependably with "This is the irrelavant question to the current given source so please ask the relavant questions" under all strategies. This validates the strict system prompt.

## 4. Technical Definition Lookup
**Query 4**:"Explain the Vector Space Model used in Information Retrieval."
**Observation**:Similarity Search performed particularly well for this query since the explanation is presented clearly within a single section of the chapter. Strategy B produced concise and accurate responses with minimal irrelevant context.


### 5. Out-of-Scope Factual Query
- **Query 5**: "Who is the CM of TamilNadu in 2026"
- **Observation**: Model refused to answer based on system prompt directive 4.

### 6. Citation Formatting Verification
- **Query 6**: "What does the author conclude about future work?"
- **Observation**: Regardless of strategy, injecting explicitly parsed `source` and `page` metadata alongside every chunk inside the LLM context allowed consistent and structured citation outputs. The model did not hallucinate page numbers.

## 7. Formula and Mathematical Retrieval
**Query 7**:"What is the BM25 scoring formula used in Information Retrieval?"
**Observation**:The markdown-based document extraction preserved mathematical equations effectively, allowing every retrieval strategy to return the formula correctly. Strategy C maintained enough surrounding explanation to help the language model interpret the equation accurately.


## 8. Dense Retrieval Methods
**Query**:"How does the ColBERT retrieval approach improve dense retrieval?"
**Observation**:All retrieval methods successfully located the ColBERT section. However, the Reranker improved the relevance of the retrieved passages, allowing the language model to generate a more detailed explanation of token-level interactions.

## Embeddings Comparison (Open-Source vs Commercial)

As part of our architectural validation, we built a UI toggle allowing instant switching between two distinct embedding models, indexing the exact same PDFs using both systems for comparison.

- **HuggingFace (`BAAI/bge-small-en`) [Open-Source]**: As a completely local baseline, `bge-small-en` effectively captured standard keyword density and performed flawlessly on semantic proximity. Best of all, it incurs zero API cost and runs entirely offline.
- **Google Gemini (`models/text-embedding-004`) [Commercial]**: Leveraging Google's state-of-the-art embedding endpoint yielded noticeably stronger performance at capturing abstract context and "dense logic" across multi-hop scenarios. Because Gemini maps embeddings across a highly complex semantic space, ambiguous queries were matched to specific architectural paragraphs vastly quicker than the local model.

---

## Conclusion
- **For most research paper question-answering scenarios**: Use **Strategy C + Hybrid Retrieval**.
- **For short, highly specific factual queries**: Use **Strategy B + Similarity Search** for precise retrieval.

*RPAnswerBot has the ability to evaluate these strategies actively in the application's sidebar.*
